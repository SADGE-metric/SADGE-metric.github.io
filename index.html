Auditing Metrics for Synthetic Data Utility: A Cross-Domain Study of Appearance and Geometry

Patryk Bartkowiak1,4, Dominik L. Michels2,4, Sören Pirk3,4, Wojtek Palubicki1,4
1Adam Mickiewicz University, 2KAUST, 3Kiel University, 4GreenMatterAI
WACV 2027
Code arXiv

Our evaluation measures the association between synthetic-data utility and two complementary signal families: appearance similarity and geometry consistency between real and synthetic images. For each real image, comparison is performed either using an aligned real–synthetic pair or by retrieving the best synthetic match from a candidate subset. We benchmark each signal independently; as a secondary analysis, a constrained bilinear interaction called SADGE probes whether the two families contain complementary information on the assembled benchmark.

Abstract

Image-similarity metrics are widely used to assess synthetic data, yet it remains unclear whether they predict how effectively synthetic training data transfer to real images. We present a common evaluation protocol for appearance- and structure-oriented proxies and apply it to seven synthetic-to-real benchmark families, 21 dataset variants, and 87,880 image-pair comparisons spanning object detection, semantic segmentation, and 6D pose estimation. No standalone metric is consistently strongest: appearance metrics are more informative in settings dominated by rendering, texture, or illumination differences, whereas correspondence-based metrics provide stronger evidence when pose, viewpoint, or spatial structure varies. These results show that the two signal families capture distinct aspects of synthetic-to-real compatibility.

As a secondary analysis, we introduce SADGE, a transparent three-parameter interaction between DINOv3 appearance similarity and MASt3R correspondence. On the complete benchmark, SADGE reaches Pearson r = 0.834 and Spearman ρ = 0.853, compared with r = 0.585 and 0.545 for the strongest appearance- and structure-oriented standalone metrics. With the component pair fixed, repeated variant-level calibration yields median held-out correlations from 0.611 to 0.755, surpassing the strongest unfitted metric from five calibration variants onward. Under exhaustive family-disjoint calibration, median held-out correlation rises from 0.529 with two calibration families to 0.841 with five, although the wider dispersion and negative tail reveal substantial sensitivity to calibration composition. Together, the benchmark and held-out analyses support evaluating appearance and structural compatibility jointly, while positioning SADGE as a calibrated training-free diagnostic for downstream validation.
